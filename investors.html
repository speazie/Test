The hard part is trust — and that's the business

Anyone can post a spare room online. What makes a live-in care arrangement actually work is everything around it. That layer is difficult to build well, which is exactly why it's valuable.

  • ✓
    Vetting. Background checks, reference checks, and in-person interviews before any caregiver is introduced to any senior.
  • ✓
    Training. Caregivers are prepared for the realities of living with and helping an older adult before they move in.
  • ✓
    Matching. Pairings based on needs, personality, and location — with in-person meetings before anyone commits to anything.
  • ✓
    Ongoing management. Regular check-ins with both sides, someone to call when things get hard, and a clear, fair way to end an arrangement.

None of this is a one-time setup cost. It is the ongoing work of the company — and every successful match deepens the know-how, reputation, and track record that anyone starting later would have to rebuild from zero.

Under development

The financial concept, in plain words

For most seniors, their home is their wealth. Today, the main ways to turn that wealth into monthly support are reverse mortgages and home-equity loans — which convert home value into bank fees and compounding interest.

TEST is designing a more direct alternative. Investors provide capital that supports the care arrangement, and in exchange the investment is intended to earn a return tied to the underlying home's value over time. The aim is simple to state: the home's value funds the owner's care, instead of leaking away in charges to a lender.

We want to be equally plain about what is not settled. The exact legal structure, how and when any return would be paid, tax treatment, and the specific protections for each party are all still being designed, and they touch regulated territory. We will not present any of it as final until it is — here or anywhere else.

Investor questions, answered straight

What stage is this really at?

Concept stage. There is no revenue, no completed matches, no pilots, and the financial structure is still being designed. What exists is the model, the thinking behind it, and the materials you're reading. We'd rather you know that from us, on page one, than discover it in diligence.

What are the main risks?

Three stand out, and we'd flag them even if you didn't ask.

Regulatory. The model sits where housing, caregiving, and home-equity finance meet — each regulated, with rules that vary by place and can change. The structure has to be designed carefully around them, and that design isn't finished.

Trust and matching. The whole model rests on matches being safe and successful. Building the vetting, training, and support to deliver that consistently is the hardest part of the business — and any serious failure would damage it.

Early stage. Our assumptions haven't been tested in the market. Plans will change, timelines are uncertain, and any eventual investment could be lost entirely.

How would investors earn a return?

The concept: investor capital supports the care arrangement, and the investment is intended to earn a return tied to the underlying home's value over time. How and when any return would be paid is not yet final, and we make no promises about outcomes. When the structure is designed and reviewed, we'll share it in full — until then we won't describe it as settled.

How is this different from a reverse mortgage?

A reverse mortgage is a loan against the home: the senior gets cash, and fees plus compounding interest flow to a lender. The TEST concept is intended to be more direct and more humane — the home's value funds care and companionship in the home itself, with the arrangement managed by people who stay involved. That's the design goal; the exact mechanics are still under development.

Couldn't seniors and caregivers just arrange this themselves?

Some do, informally — and when it goes well, it's wonderful. But without screening, training, clear agreements, and someone to call when things get hard, the risk falls entirely on two strangers. That trust layer is precisely what most people can't build on their own, and it's the product.

Can I invest right now?

No. Nothing on this page is an offer, and there is nothing to buy today. What you can do is tell us you're interested — we'll start a conversation, keep you informed as the structure takes shape, and come back to you when there is something concrete to consider.
