Is this right for you — or your parent?

TEST is for seniors who want to keep living in their own homes and could use another pair of hands — and for the sons and daughters helping them work out how.

  • You (or your mom or dad) want to stay home, not move to a facility.
  • The house has a spare room — and lately it feels a little too quiet, or a little too much to keep up alone.
  • Everyday things like cooking, cleaning, errands, and rides are getting harder.
  • The home is worth a good deal, but the monthly budget is tight — and paying for full-time professional care would stretch it past breaking.
  • You'd welcome the company as much as the help.

If several of those sound familiar, this page was written for you. And if your very first thought is "How could I ever trust a stranger in my home?" — good. That's the right question, and it's the one we take most seriously. Skip ahead to how we keep you safe.

What a live-in caregiver helps with

Every arrangement is shaped around what you actually need — no more, no less. The help ranges from friendly support around the house to more involved personal care, and it's always matched to what the caregiver is trained and qualified to do.

🍲

Everyday life

Cooking and shared meals, light housekeeping, laundry, errands, rides to appointments — and a hand with the garden, the dog, or whatever your days include.

☕

Companionship

Conversation over coffee, a walk around the block, someone who notices how you're doing. For many seniors this matters most of all — and it's a quiet safeguard too, because someone is simply there.

🤲

Personal care, when needed

For seniors who need more, some caregivers are qualified to help with things like bathing, dressing, and moving around the house safely. More involved care is only ever matched with caregivers trained for it.


What caregivers are not: they are not nurses, and they don't replace medical care. Your doctors and nurses stay exactly who they are. A caregiver can help with medication reminders and rides to appointments — but medical needs stay with medical professionals, full stop.

How we keep you safe

Letting someone new into your home is a big decision, and we built TEST around that fact. Nothing moves forward until you — and your family, if you want them involved — feel genuinely comfortable. Here's what stands between you and a stranger at the door.

  • ✓
    Screening comes first. Every caregiver passes background checks, reference checks, and in-person interviews before they are ever introduced to anyone.
  • ✓
    Trained before they arrive. Caregivers complete training for the kind of help they'll provide, and are only matched to needs they're prepared to meet.
  • ✓
    You choose, at your own pace. Meet a suggested caregiver as many times as you like, wherever you're comfortable. Nobody moves in until you say yes.
  • ✓
    Everything in writing. A clear, plain-language agreement spells out the help provided, the housing offered, and how either side can end the arrangement.
  • ✓
    We stay involved. TEST isn't a website that makes an introduction and disappears. We check in regularly with you and your caregiver, and you always have a real person to call.
  • ✓
    Family in the loop. Your adult children — or anyone you trust — can be part of every step: the first meeting, the paperwork, the ongoing check-ins.

A gentler way to afford care

Many seniors are in the same spot: the house is worth a good deal, but the monthly income is modest. "House rich, cash poor," as the saying goes.

Professional care is expensive and hard to find. A room in a care facility can cost [average monthly facility cost figure], and hiring full-time help at home often costs even more — enough to drain careful savings in a few short years.

TEST starts from a simpler trade. Your home has something a good caregiver genuinely needs: a place to live. The caregiver has something you need: help and company. Because housing is a real part of how the caregiver is compensated, this arrangement can cost far less than a facility or full-time hired care.

Beyond that, we're designing a way for the value of your home to help pay for care — so that care is funded by the home you already own rather than by your limited monthly cash. You may have heard of reverse mortgages or home-equity loans, where a bank advances money against your home and, over the years, fees and interest eat into what it's worth. Our aim is more direct and more humane: instead of your home's value flowing to a bank, it goes toward actual care, delivered in your own home, by someone you know.

We want to be straight with you: this financial side of TEST is still being designed, and the details matter. We won't quote numbers we can't stand behind, and we'll never rush you.

Before you decide anything, we'll sit down with you — and your family or your own advisor, if you wish — and walk through exactly how it would work for your situation, in plain language, on paper. Nothing on this page is a quote, a guarantee, or financial or legal advice; honest answers depend on your circumstances.

How it works, step by step

From first hello to settled in, here's what to expect. You can pause, slow down, or stop at any point along the way.

  1. Say hello

    Tell us a little about yourself — or have a son or daughter do it for you. There's no cost and no obligation.

  2. Talk it through

    We listen and learn: what kind of help would make life easier, what your home is like, and what kind of person you'd enjoy having around.

  3. Meet your matches

    We introduce carefully screened caregivers who fit your needs. Meet as often as you like, with family present if you wish. It only goes forward if it feels right to you.

  4. Put it in writing

    Together we set out the help, the housing, and the ground rules in a clear agreement that everyone understands and signs — including how either side can end it.

  5. Settle in, with backup

    Your caregiver moves in and daily life gets easier. We check in regularly with both of you, and we're a phone call away whenever something needs adjusting.

Questions seniors and families ask us

Who exactly would be living in my home?

One caregiver — a real person you choose, not someone assigned to you. Before anything is decided, they've passed background checks, reference checks, and in-person interviews, and completed training. You meet them as many times as you like, with family present if you wish. Nobody is ever placed in your home; you invite them.

Do I give up my home, or any say over it?

No. It's your home and you keep living in it — that's the whole point. The caregiver gets an agreed place to live there, spelled out in writing. If the value of your home helps pay for care, how that works is explained openly, in plain language, before you sign anything — and we encourage you to have family or your own independent advisor look everything over first.

Is the caregiver a nurse?

No, and we're upfront about that. Caregivers help with daily life — meals, the house, errands, companionship, and for some qualified caregivers, personal care like bathing and dressing. They don't provide medical care and they don't replace your doctors or nurses. They can help with rides to appointments and medication reminders.

What does it cost?

Housing is a real part of the caregiver's compensation, which is why this can cost far less than a facility or full-time hired care. The honest answer beyond that depends on your situation and how much help you need. We'll walk you through the actual numbers for your circumstances before you decide anything — no figure on this page is a quote or a promise.

What if we just don't get along?

Then it ends — kindly and clearly. Every agreement includes a fair way for either side to step away. And because we check in regularly, small frictions usually get talked through early. If it truly isn't working, we help with next steps, which can include a different match.

Can my family be involved?

As involved as you'd like. Many families join from the very first conversation and stay part of the regular check-ins. Some seniors prefer to handle things themselves — that's fine too. It's your call.

Do I have to decide quickly?

No. There are no deadlines and no "limited spots." Take the weeks you need, talk it over with your family, and ask us the same question five times if you want to. This only works if you're genuinely comfortable.
