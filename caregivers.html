What you actually get

Four things. All of them real, and all of them written into your agreement before you move in.

🏡

A place to live

Your own private room in a senior's home — a home we've visited ourselves. Depending on the arrangement, housing is free or offered at a reduced cost, with the terms set down in writing before you commit to anything.

💰

Compensation on top

Housing is part of the deal — never the whole deal. You're compensated for the care you provide, in addition to your housing. The exact structure depends on the arrangement, and it's settled in your written agreement before you move in.

🎓

Training you keep

We train you for the care you'll actually be providing, and keep supporting you as you're ready to grow into more. Skills like these stay with you, wherever life takes you next.

📞

Support that stays

You get a support contact at TEST, regular check-ins, and help coordinating with the senior and their family. We stay involved for the life of the arrangement — you're never left to figure things out alone.

Is this you?

You don't need a caregiving résumé — training is our job. What we look for is character. Caregivers who do well here tend to be:

  • ✓
    Dependable. You show up when you say you will. Someone will genuinely count on you, and that's a responsibility you take seriously.
  • ✓
    Patient and kind. Good days and slower days — you can meet both with warmth, without needing everything to go to plan.
  • ✓
    A decent communicator. You can say what you need, hear what someone else needs, and raise a problem before it grows.
  • ✓
    Ready to share a home. Living alongside someone from a different generation takes respect, curiosity, and a sense of humor.

Students, career-changers, people saving toward a place of their own — there's no single "right" background for this. If the list above sounds like you, we'd like to hear from you.

What a week can look like

Care spans a spectrum, and you're matched to the level you're qualified for and comfortable with — never beyond it. Here's the honest picture.

☕

Companionship

Shared meals, conversation, a walk around the block, someone noticing how the day is going. It sounds simple — and it's often the part that matters most to the senior you live with.

🧺

Help around the house

Cooking, errands, light housekeeping, rides to appointments, reminders. Everyday things that get harder with age and that you can genuinely take off someone's plate.

🌱

More involved care

Some arrangements include more hands-on personal care — but only if you're trained and qualified for it, and only if it's agreed in writing. This is never medical or nursing work.

Two things to be clear-eyed about. First, this is real responsibility, not a rent hack — a person is relying on you, and the arrangement only works if you mean it. Second, it is not around-the-clock duty: your agreement spells out what's expected of you, and outside of that, your time is your own.

Is this fair to you? Here's how we make sure.

"Care in exchange for housing" can go badly when it's vague or one-sided — we know that's the first thing a smart person checks. So TEST is built so the arrangement can't quietly become that. Every match comes with:

  • ✓
    A written agreement. The care expected, the housing provided, your compensation, and how either side can end the arrangement — in plain language, signed by everyone before you move in.
  • ✓
    Agreed limits. What's expected of you is defined and bounded. You are not on call around the clock, and "just a bit more" is never assumed — it's either in the agreement or it isn't expected.
  • ✓
    Your own private room. Space that is yours, agreed up front. You're a member of the household, not staff quartered in it.
  • ✓
    Real pay, not just a room. Housing never substitutes for compensation. You receive both, and both are in the agreement.
  • ✓
    Vetting that goes both ways. Yes, we screen you — and the senior side is screened too, and the home visited, before you're ever introduced. You are not sent blind into an unknown situation.
  • ✓
    Check-ins and someone to call. We check in with you regularly after you move in, and you have a support contact at TEST for any question, concern, or thing that just feels off.
  • ✓
    A fair way out. If it isn't working, the agreement gives both sides a clear, fair path to end the arrangement — and TEST stays involved rather than leaving you to sort it out alone.

How it works, from your side

Five steps from "curious" to "moved in" — and you can step away at any point along the way.

  1. Tell us about yourself

    A short application: who you are, your situation, what kind of care you're comfortable providing, and where you could live. No caregiving experience required to apply.

  2. Screening

    Background checks, reference checks, and interviews. We're careful on both sides — the seniors and homes you could be matched with go through their own screening too.

  3. Training

    Before you're matched, we train you for the level of care you'll actually provide — and that training keeps building as you grow.

  4. Meet and match

    We suggest a senior based on needs, personality, and location. You meet in person, see the home and your room, and take your time. Either side can pass — no pressure, no penalty.

  5. Move in, with backup

    Once everyone has signed the agreement, you move in. Regular check-ins and your TEST support contact continue for as long as the arrangement does.

Questions caregivers ask us

Is the housing actually real?

Yes — it's the foundation of the whole model, not a perk bolted on. The home exists before you're matched: it's a real senior's real home, which TEST has visited. Your private room and the housing terms are written into the agreement you sign, and you'll see the home yourself before you decide anything.

Isn't this just cheap labor in exchange for a room?

That's the right question to ask, and the model is designed so the answer is no. You're compensated for the care you provide on top of your housing — a room never replaces pay. What's expected of you is defined and limited in a written agreement, not open-ended. And if an arrangement starts asking more of you than what was agreed, you have a support contact at TEST and a fair way to end it. If any of those pieces were missing, we wouldn't ask you to sign.

Will I have my own space?

Yes. Every arrangement includes your own private room, agreed up front and written into the agreement. You're sharing a home, not sleeping on a couch.

How much care will be expected of me?

An agreed amount — no more. Care ranges from companionship and help around the house up to more involved personal care, and you're matched to the level you're trained and comfortable with. Your agreement spells out what's expected, you're not on call around the clock, and your time outside those responsibilities is your own.

Do I need experience or certifications?

No professional caregiving experience is required to apply. TEST trains you for the care you'll provide, and this is not medical or nursing work — that stays with medical professionals. What we can't teach is dependability, patience, and kindness, so that's what we look for.

How do I know I'll be safe in someone else's home?

Because vetting runs in both directions. The senior side is screened and the home visited before any introduction, you meet the senior and see the home before committing, and after you move in there are regular check-ins and a TEST support contact you can reach whenever something doesn't feel right.

What if it doesn't work out?

Every agreement includes a clear, fair way for either side — including you — to end the arrangement. Sometimes a match just isn't right, and that's not a failure. TEST stays involved throughout, so if things aren't working you have someone to call, and you won't be handling the ending on your own.
